PRESS LOG: Public Information

CHANNEL #1: TELEVISION BROADCASTS
Source: CBS, NBC, ABC
Reach: National

News anchors broke regular programming
to issue dire warnings.

Walter Cronkite and local anchors
instructed Americans to go into their
medicine cabinets and flush Tylenol
capsules down the toilet immediately.

Graphic footage of victims being
loaded into ambulances created a
palpable sense of terror across the
United States.

---

CHANNEL #2: NEWSPAPER HEADLINES
Source: Chicago Tribune, NY Times
Tone: Alarmist

Headlines included "Tylenol Killer"
and "Poison Madness."

The print media focused heavily on the
mystery of the perpetrator. Without a
suspect, the press speculated wildly:
- Disgruntled employees
- Corporate sabotage
- Copycat killers

This coverage pressured the police to
find a suspect quickly, leading to the
intense scrutiny of James Lewis.

---

CHANNEL #3: CORPORATE COMMUNICATION
Source: Johnson and Johnson
Strategy: Transparency

Unlike previous corporate scandals,
the manufacturer chose total honesty.

They held daily press conferences.
They did not hide the death toll.
They introduced the triple-seal
tamper-resistant packaging on live
television to restore trust.

This media strategy is now taught in
business schools as the gold standard
for crisis management.
